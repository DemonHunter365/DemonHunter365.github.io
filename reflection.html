Looking back on Year 1

When I started my BSc in Computer Science, I had curiosity but not yet the tools to act on it effectively. The first semester forced me to build those tools quickly learning to read error messages instead of panicking, to search documentation instead of guessing, and to break a problem down before writing a single line of code.

Semester 1 felt like laying foundations. The scavenger hunt taught me that working well in a team is a skill you have to practise, not something that just happens. The MARIE Assembly project showed me that computers are not magic they are just very fast at following very precise instructions. That shift in perspective changed how I think about every layer above it.

Semester 2 felt like actually building something. The Monte Carlo simulation project was the most technically demanding thing I had done — combining statistics, simulation logic, and data analysis. The Online Grocery System was the most satisfying,it was the first time I could show someone something and have them actually use it.

The human side of technology surprised me most. The CRM role play and team building activities were not what I expected from a Computer Science course but they were some of the most useful sessions of the year. Being able to communicate clearly about what you are building, and why, turns out to matter as much as the code itself.

If I could do Year 1 again, I would start personal projects earlier. Coursework teaches you the concepts, but building something for yourself something with no rubric is where the real learning happens. That is the habit I want to carry into Year 2.
